Shamir's scheme: for every byte, pick a random polynomial of degree k−1 whose constant term is that byte, and hand out its value at x = 1, 2, … n. Any k points pin the curve down (Lagrange interpolation) and its value at x = 0 is the byte. With k−1 points the curve can still pass through every possible value at x = 0 — the picture sweeps through them — so fewer than k shares carry no information at all. The real arithmetic happens in the finite field GF(2⁸), not on the real line, which is what makes "no information" exact rather than approximate.

Quorum

Split a password, recovery phrase or safe combination into n shares so that any k of them rebuild it and k−1 reveal nothing (Shamir's secret sharing over GF(256), with crypto.getRandomValues). Give the cards to different people or places; combine them when needed. Each share carries a checksum, so a mistyped share is reported rather than silently wrong, and spare shares are cross-checked against the rest.

Everything runs in this one file, offline. Save stores the shares, label and settings — never the plaintext secret. Save downloads a file when the page is opened directly from disk; the artifact viewer blocks page downloads.
