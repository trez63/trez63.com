    Trill — a modem made of birdsong

    Trill turns text into a short melody of 32 pure tones (2000–3938 Hz) and decodes it again from any microphone. Nothing leaves the page: no network, no server, no library. Each 4-bit nibble is one tone; consecutive symbols alternate between two interleaved tone banks so every boundary is audible to the decoder, which uses Hann-windowed Goertzel filters, finds a 6-tone preamble with a matched filter (learning the speed from it), keeps its symbol clock aligned with an early/late gate, repairs up to 2 wrong tones in every 15 with a Reed–Solomon code, then checks a CRC-8.

    Phone tips: raise the volume and turn the ringer on. Hold devices within a metre. Microphone access needs a secure page (https) or a desktop file; iPhone Chrome asks once.
